# CRISPR Experiment Design: CFTR F508del Correction

## 1. Research objective

The objective of the study was to investigate whether CRISPR-Cas9 could correct the F508del mutation in the CFTR gene in cells from people with cystic fibrosis.

The F508del mutation involves the deletion of three DNA bases (CTT), resulting in the loss of the phenylalanine amino acid at position 508 of the CFTR protein.

## 2. CRISPR-Cas9 approach

The researchers investigated different CRISPR-Cas9 combinations to target the mutated CFTR gene.

The systems included:

- SpCas9
- SpCas9(HF4)
- SaCas9

Each system was paired with a guide RNA (sgRNA) designed to recognise a specific target sequence in the CFTR gene.

## 3. DNA repair strategy

After Cas9 cuts the target DNA, the cell can repair the break.

The study used a single-stranded oligodeoxynucleotide (ssODN) repair template. This template provided the sequence needed to restore the missing three DNA bases.

The intended correction relied on a repair process called **homology-directed repair (HDR)**.

## 4. Experimental comparison

The researchers compared different combinations of Cas9 proteins and guide RNAs.

They measured HDR-related editing outcomes and examined whether the intended genetic correction had occurred.

The results allowed the researchers to compare which CRISPR combination performed best under the tested conditions.

## 5. Main result from my data analysis

Among the combinations recorded in my results table, SpCas9(HF4) with sg#1 showed the highest precise genomic HDR value: 49.63% relative to NHEJ.

This value should not be interpreted as 49.63% of all cells being corrected. The study also reported much lower overall HDR frequencies in the tested cell populations.

## 6. Scope and limitations

This project is a computational and literature-based replication. I analysed the published experimental design and results; I did not perform CRISPR editing or laboratory experiments myself.

The results are limited by the efficiency of HDR and the specific cell types and experimental conditions used in the original study.
